This Week 1 report develops a robust experimental design plan based on an extensive literature review in a selected area of biotechnology, and it focuses on identified gaps in biotechnological product development, covering every requirement of the task. The product chosen is polyhydroxyalkanoate (PHA) bioplastic, and the intervention is placed at the carbon feedstock rather than at the polymer chemistry, because the economics of the product are decided there: PHA costs roughly US$4 to US$6 per kg to manufacture against US$1.1 to US$1.4 per kg for the polypropylene and PET it must displace, and the carbon substrate accounts for up to half of that cost. The literature review is structured around four questions that determine whether the product can work, and it draws on fourteen reputable sources, exceeding the ten required. It establishes that volatile fatty acids derived from the dark fermentation of food waste are cheap, metabolically proximate to PHA, capable of supporting PHA contents up to 91 per cent of dry cell weight, and uniquely able to let the polymer's monomer composition be set by the composition of the feed, while also being toxic above roughly 5 grams per litre. Three specific gaps are identified: the tolerance-versus-titre trade-off is unresolved on real mixed feeds, the adaptive-laboratory-evolution mutations that improve VFA growth have never been tested on one, and predictive composition control from feed composition has not been demonstrated. The experimental design uses Cupriavidus necator H16 with five strain backgrounds, six carbon sources and three VFA ratios in a two-stage programme screening ninety flask conditions before controlled fed-batch bioreactor runs, with materials, methods and procedures set out in full. A clear research question and three hypotheses are defined, anticipated outcomes are stated with quantitative targets, and six potential challenges are paired with specific troubleshooting strategies. A target titre of 45 grams per litre is set deliberately below the best published purified-substrate result, and the interpretation rules are fixed in advance so the study can fail honestly.
