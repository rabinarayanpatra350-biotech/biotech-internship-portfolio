# Internship Overview

**Biotechnology Research Internship** — Rabi Narayan Patra — 26 September to 31 October 2026

Five weeks, each producing a distinct deliverable, all directed at one biotechnological product problem.

## The problem

Polyhydroxyalkanoate (PHA) is a genuinely good material: fully biodegradable, biocompatible, made from
renewable carbon, and already commercialised. It is also uncompetitive on price. PHA costs roughly
**US$4–6 per kg** to manufacture against **US$1.1–1.4 per kg** for polypropylene and PET, and the carbon
feedstock alone accounts for **up to half** of that cost.

A material that is functionally superior and economically three to four times more expensive does not displace
an incumbent. It occupies a niche — and the environmental problem it was meant to address continues largely
unaddressed. The internship began from that arithmetic and from one observation about it: **the product is won
or lost at the feedstock, not at the polymer chemistry.**

## The answer pursued

Food waste is the feedstock chosen, and the reasoning is economic before it is biological. Food waste is not a
commodity with a market price; it is a **cost to the site generating it**. The EU alone produces around
**132 kg per inhabitant per year, some 60 million tonnes**. It is also 75–80% water, which makes it expensive
to transport and therefore ideal to process where it arises.

Converting it requires two biological stages operating under opposite conditions: a non-sterile acidogenic
community that converts the waste to volatile fatty acids, and an axenic culture of engineered
*Cupriavidus necator* that polymerises those acids into intracellular polyester. The two cannot be run
together, and that constraint shapes the entire design.

## Week by week

### Week 1 — Experimental Design and Literature Review
A fourteen-source review establishing the cost structure and the feedstock's share of it, choosing the
feedstock and organism, and identifying three specific gaps. **Handed on:** the feedstock, the organism, and the
three gaps the rest of the internship had to live with.

### Week 2 — Laboratory Methodologies and Data Collection Simulation
A five-part laboratory procedure from cryostock to a validated polymer titre, with a full safety assessment and
a quality-control framework built on two analytical methods that share no steps. **Handed on:** the number
**5 g/L** — the concentration above which VFA feeds inhibit the production strain. A qualitative difficulty
produces no design; a number produces a specification.

### Week 3 — Bioinformatics Data Analysis and Interpretation
A complete pipeline on eighteen real PHA synthase sequences across four classes and an archaeal outgroup.
All five groups recovered as monophyletic clades in 100% of bootstrap replicates; 15 invariant columns; an
invariant catalytic cysteine. **Handed on:** a class-specific lipase-box signature — **G-[FY]-C-[VI]-G-G**,
**G-A-C-A-G-G**, **G-[IV]-C-Q-G-G**, **G-Y-C-M-G-G** — that predicts substrate range from seven residues.

### Week 4 — Prototype Development and Findings Documentation
A modular, transportable unit converting food waste into PHA at the point of generation, with four modules and
a pH-static control loop holding the reactor at **1 g/L**. Projected output **1.13 kg/day** of dry polymer from
**20 kg/day** of dry waste — a 5.7% overall mass yield, stated plainly. Twelve failure modes scored before
mitigation; the highest-scoring ones are economic rather than technical.

### Week 5 — Final Integration, Reporting and Presentation
An integrated report consolidating the four components, tracing ten design decisions to their sources, stating
the integration method explicitly, and reporting the principal failure in full — accompanied by a
fourteen-slide briefing deck with speaker notes on every slide.

## The arc

The five weeks form a single argument rather than five topics:

**cost problem → quantified constraint → enzyme evidence → design → synthesis**

Each step narrows the space available to the next. Week 1 decided the feedstock and the organism; Week 2
quantified the obstacle that feedstock creates; Week 3 identified which enzyme suits which feed; Week 4
honoured all three; Week 5 tested whether the chain actually holds — and found one place where a later week
improved an earlier one's reasoning.

## Honest assessment

The science is at **moderate readiness**: the unit's individual operations are all established, but their
integration is not. The economics are the **critical unknown** — a commercial assumption rather than a
technical one. The recovery module has the **weakest published evidence** behind it. And one method failed
outright and is reported in full rather than omitted.

What the internship produced that stands regardless of whether the process is ever built: the cost structure
documented with sources, a quantified design constraint, a usable sequence-screening result, and a design
principle that generalises beyond PHA.
